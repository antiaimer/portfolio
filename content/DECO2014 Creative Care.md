# Context
## The Brief
We were asked to design interactive experiences around care as an emotional, physical, social, and ethical practice to use digital technologies to deepen empathy, connection, and wellbeing for [[DECO2014]].

We were to chose a specific care context and user group, identify unmet care needs or challenges, and create a concept that integrates the digital, physical, and spatial/processual elements into a holistic user experience. 

## My Role
In this assignment we (group-mates and I) all participated in the background research and 

- Contributed to background research to understand how women currently navigate safety on public transport.
- Helped define the problem space and unmet needs 
	- (Fear at night, lack of trusted support, difficulty reporting or signalling for help)
- Defined user journey from primary data
- Made the physical prototype with Justin Ma
- Took part in the User Testing Fair
	- Gathered feedback
	- Observed how users interacted with the concept
	- Translated the insights into further design iteration proposals

# Intent
## Women's Safety on Public Transport
For this brief, we explored **Women's Safety on Public Transport** as a form of care, and to design an experience that supports women emotionally and physically throughout their journeys. 

Our research showed that women using public transport have the burden of keeping themselves safe, adjusting routes, behaviour, and emotions to avoid risk.

![[DECO2014 Creative Care-1779082560264.webp]]

Through the Brief, the goal was to utilise digital, physical and spatial / processual interfaces to make women feel safer, more supported, and more connected.


# Moves
## Early Exploration and Storyboarding
As part of [[DECO2014]]'s A2 assessment, we developed storyboards for potential design solutions, exploring how we might be able to develop a concept which allow women to feel more safe.

## Version 1.0

![[DECO2014 Creative Care-1779102405432.webp]]

We decided to try to utilise Jade's storyboard as the base for the design concept.  Her concept was a "buddy matching" app system which aimed to allow women travelling alone to match with another.

## Version 1.1

After consideration within the heuristic evaluations, we decided that we needed to include a more spatial aspect. During the class I developed a mockup of a train station platform in blender which had included an area that lights up to allow for those who matched to discretely find each-other.

![[DECO2014 Creative Care-1779102706943.webp]]

![[DECO2014 Creative Care-1779102717681.webp]]

## Version 1.2
In the studio, we found that there may be issues with this design concept.
- How can we prove that they are real women?
- What if someone misuses the system?
These issues highlighted risk around identity verification, safety, and exclusion.
	To solve this, we discussed the possibility of ID verification for the users as a layer of safety, where users would sign up to the app and take a photo of themselves and their valid ID photo to use the service.

## Version 2.0
After later discussion with our tutor, we decided to integrate a more spatial aspect to the design. We expanded into **safe waiting zones** and **kiosks** layered onto the existing app idea.

From this idea, we decided to create a cardboard version of our design for user testing.

![[DECO2014 Creative Care-1779109243522.webp]]

## Version 2.1
We conducted a few think aloud tests to see where this design may fall short. 
For the app we found that our app had too many text inputs.

![[DECO2014 Creative Care-1779109397464.webp]]

## User Testing Fair
At the fair, we used the cardboard model with the marked safe waiting zones and a scenario to observe where participants would stand and move when issues would arise. 
We utilised the think aloud test, as well as an interview and the SUS scores to gauge how they perceived the idea's usability.

# Solution
## What Next?
Our current solution is a **Safe Waiting Zone** combined with a **kiosk/app** interface. The spatial layer uses clearly marked zones positioned where lighting, sight-lines, and staff provide maximum real-time visibility, whilst the digital layer lets users match with others going on the same train line.

Rather than a finished product, this mid-fidelity concept can be improved with the addition of:
- Card integrations for kiosk top-on sign in.
- Widening the zone towards the wall.
- Demographic matching by age
- Fallback instructions
# Reflection
For this project, I believe that my group did well with ideation, but were somewhat naive with how we started. I believe our addition to the idea, aided by our tutors feedback, better reflected how we can aid in women's safety on public transport. Our iterations were documented and were informed by user testing.

## What I Could've Done Better
I could've refined the user testing plan further to get more valuable information, prioritising key questions, and tightening the scenarios further with the physical prototype. Our participant group should've been better with participants being in a more diverse group of women. Future iterations should've been defined earlier so we could've partially prototyped it.

## What I Learned
I learned how to develop a concrete design space from an ambiguous care problem. I gained experience in physical prototyping and user testing.

## What I Did Well
I contributed strongly to the development and documentation of the design, as I was in charge of both appendixes. I developed the user testing plan and conducted most of the tests.
